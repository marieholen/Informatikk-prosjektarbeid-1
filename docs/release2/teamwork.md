# Team Coordination and Project Experience
In this release, we focused more on agile development, mainly following the Scrum methodology while also applying some Extreme Programming practices.

## Project Experience

### Scrum
At the beginning of release 2, we chose Elias as our Scrum Master. He was responsible for communicating with our teaching assistant (Knut, the Product Owner) whenever the team had questions about the application, as well as facilitating our weekly meetings. We did not have daily Scrum meetings due to our different schedules, but we used our Messenger group actively whenever questions occurred.

In release 2, we had three sprints. Sprint 1 and 2 lasted about seven days each, while sprint 3 lasted five days including the weekend. Sprint 1 and 2 involved implementing core functionality for the application, while sprint 3 focused mainly on documentation. During the sprints, we noticed that some team members did not complete their assigned issues on time, so it affected the time on later sprints.

We created a Scrum board on GitHub to visualize and organize our issues systematically, moving them through the workflow as needed. The issue board included the following columns: Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, In Progress, In Review, and Done. We also had a Testing column, but halfway through release 2 we realized that testing was already covered during the review process.

When estimating issues, we used verbal planning poker. We discussed the estimated size of each issue in T-shirt sizes. If there were disagreements, the team members who disagreed could explain their reasoning before we voted again. The challenge with verbal planning poker is that people can be easily influenced by others’ opinions, but given our limited time, we felt this was the best approach for us.

### Extreme Programming
- Ella and Cathrine practiced pair programming while working on the persistence files. However, due to different schedules, it was difficult to maintain throughout the rest of the release.
- Marie and Elias also practiced pair programming on User.java and Recipe.java.
- Everyone practiced collective code ownership by allowing any team member to modify any part of the code. Every member was responsible for maintaining code quality.

### Issue Distribution and Planning
During sprints 1 and 2, issues were prioritized based on importance. The higher the priority, the sooner the issue needed to be completed. Since many issues depended on one another, the high-priority tasks had to be completed first. Team members assigned issues to themselves until all tasks were taken.
In sprint 3, we noticed that this self-assignment approach lacked structure — not all issues were taken, and some members ended up with more work than others. During the sprint 3 planning meeting, when everyone was present, we decided to distribute the tasks more evenly across the team.

### Improvements for Future Collaboration
Throughout the project, we learned the importance of clear communication and early feedback to avoid misunderstandings. We also realized that all issues in an ongoing sprint should be assigned while everyone is present to ensure a fair and balanced workload.

As this was our first experience using Scrum, we now have a better understanding of how to apply it more effectively next time. Later we wish for more structure in assigning the issues.
